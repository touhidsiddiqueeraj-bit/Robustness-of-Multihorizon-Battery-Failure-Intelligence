Motivation

Lab results don't travel

The published hazard model was trained on clean lab data: full cycles, fixed C-rates, controlled temperatures.

Real BESS operation is fundamentally different:

  • Partial cycling — varying depths of discharge
  • Temperature noise — sensor drift, thermal gradients
  • Irregular rest — variable idle between cycles
⚠

Does calibration survive
when the model meets real data?

Approach

Systematic robustness test
across four severity levels

Diagnosis

Root cause: Vmin shift

The primary driver is a shift in the minimum voltage feature under partial cycling.

2.3 V
Full discharge
3.2 V
75% DoD truncation

A 39% increase — the XGBoost model learned thresholds on clean ranges; the isotonic calibrator can't adapt to the shifted distribution.

Three operating zones

● Safe

ECE < 0.05
Clean lab conditions only

● Warning

ECE 0.05–0.10
Recalibrated operation achievable
with 10% operational sample

● Unsafe

ECE > 0.10
Direct deployment under any
partial cycling (ECE > 0.27)

Direct deployment under partial cycling pushes ECE > 0.27 — firmly in the unsafe zone

Action

Practical deployment path

1

Deploy the frozen XGBoost model as-is

2

Collect 50–100 operational cycles
from normal BESS operation

3

Fit a new isotonic regression on these cycles

4

Deploy the recalibrated model
No base classifier retraining • No original training data needed

operational sample suffices

Takeaway

Calibration is fragile.
Recalibration is practical.

Multihorizon hazard models are not inherently robust to
operational distribution shift, but a lightweight recalibration
step suffices for field deployment.
